When Can You Drink Alcohol After a Colonoscopy?

The short answer: Wait at least 24 hours. MedlinePlus colonoscopy guidance says to avoid alcohol for at least 24 hours after the test, because the sedatives are still in your system. If you feel normal the next day and had no polyps removed, a drink is usually fine. After polyp removal, ask your endoscopy team before drinking.

Why do you have to wait 24 hours to drink after a colonoscopy?

The wait comes from the sedatives, not the scope. Alcohol is a central nervous system depressant, and so are the drugs used for colonoscopy sedation. Taken together, their effects stack.

The FDA labels spell this out:

  • Midazolam. The midazolam injection label warns that alcohol and other depressants "may increase the risk of hypoventilation, airway obstruction, desaturation, or apnea" and "may contribute to profound and/or prolonged drug effect." It also notes that alcohol has an increased effect when taken with benzodiazepines.
  • Fentanyl. The fentanyl citrate injection label carries a boxed warning. Use with benzodiazepines or other depressants, "including alcohol," may cause profound sedation, slowed breathing, coma, and death.

You might feel alert an hour after the procedure. The drug can still be there. The midazolam label lists an elimination half-life of 1.8 to 6.4 hours in healthy adults, with a mean of about 3 hours. In older adults the half-life was about twice as long. The same label says no driving until drowsiness has passed or until 1 full day after the procedure, whichever is longer. Standard discharge guidance uses the same 1-day window for alcohol.

The rule also protects you from yourself. Sedation blurs memory and judgment, so a drink that evening can feel weaker than it is. That is one reason the University of Tennessee Medical Center discharge sheet lists "Do not drink alcohol today" next to "no driving" and "do not sign any important or legally binding papers today."

Does the wait change if you had propofol instead of twilight sedation?

Propofol wears off faster than midazolam. Even so, the propofol label gives no safe number of hours. It says activities that need mental alertness "may be impaired for some time after general anesthesia or sedation." The label does not set a specific alcohol rule.

Some units combine propofol with other sedatives or pain drugs. You may not know the exact mix you got. For that reason, most hospitals give everyone the same 24-hour instruction. If you had no sedation at all, the sedative reason goes away. Ask the team whether any other limits apply to you.

Should you wait longer if polyps were removed or a biopsy was taken?

This is where the answer gets less certain. Removing a polyp leaves a small wound in the colon wall. NIDDK says light bleeding is normal after a polyp removal or biopsy. It also says delayed bleeding can occur up to 2 weeks after the procedure.

We did not find a guideline from a medical society that sets a longer alcohol ban after polyp removal. The bleeding risk factors in research are about the polyp and the procedure. A 2022 study of 581 patients in Zeitschrift für Gastroenterologie linked bleeding to the number of polyps removed, polyp size, shape and location, and the removal method.

What doctors do restrict after polyp removal is often aspirin and anti-inflammatory drugs. The University of Tennessee Medical Center sheet says that, if your doctor recommends it, avoid aspirin and arthritis medicines for 7 to 10 days after biopsies or polypectomies. If a large polyp was removed, or clips were placed, ask your endoscopy team whether you should hold off on alcohol too. Keep any drinking light during the 2-week window, so you can notice warning signs. Our guide to blood in stool after polyp removal covers how much bleeding is normal.

Why does bowel prep make the first drink hit harder?

Bowel prep pulls a lot of water out of your body. The SUPREP Bowel Prep Kit label warns about serious loss of body fluid and changes in blood salts. It tells patients to hydrate adequately before, during, and after the prep. It also says not to drink alcohol while taking the prep and until after the colonoscopy.

Signs of dehydration listed on that label include vomiting, urinating less often than normal, dizziness, and headache. You also start the day after the procedure with little food in your system. A drink on top of that is a poor idea.

Before your first drink, rehydrate and eat a real meal. Water, broth, and sports drinks are good choices. If you still feel dizzy or have not urinated normally, wait longer.

Can you drink if you are taking pain medicine afterward?

Most people need no pain medicine after a colonoscopy. Gas cramps are common after the procedure. Our post on pain and bloating after colonoscopy explains what is normal.

If you do take an over-the-counter pain reliever, read its alcohol warning:

  • Acetaminophen (Tylenol). The Tylenol Extra Strength label warns that severe liver damage may occur if you have 3 or more alcoholic drinks every day while using the product.
  • Ibuprofen (Advil) and other NSAIDs. The Advil label carries a stomach bleeding warning. The risk is higher if you have 3 or more alcoholic drinks every day, or take a blood thinner. After polyp removal, your doctor may also tell you to skip NSAIDs for several days.

If you were sent home with a prescription opioid or a sleep aid, do not combine it with alcohol. The same boxed warning about depressants applies.

What does a safe first 72 hours look like?

This timeline follows the sources above. Your discharge sheet wins if it says something different.

  1. Hours 0 to 24. No alcohol. No driving and no big decisions. Sip fluids, then eat light food once you feel ready.
  2. Hours 24 to 48. If you had no polyps removed and feel fully alert, the 24-hour alcohol limit in the MedlinePlus guidance has passed. Your own discharge sheet still comes first. Drink water first. Skip it if you are still dizzy or have taken acetaminophen or ibuprofen in large amounts.
  3. Hours 48 to 72. Most people are back to normal. If polyps were removed, keep drinking light and follow any instructions about aspirin, NSAIDs, or blood thinners.
  4. Days 3 to 14 after polyp removal. Watch for delayed bleeding. MedlinePlus discharge instructions say to call your provider for black, tarry stools, blood in the stool for more than 2 bowel movements, or severe belly pain.

If you drink regularly, the end of a screening visit is also a good time to look at your habits. thrive.md covers everyday health topics, including cutting back on alcohol.

The bottom line

Wait at least 24 hours after a sedated colonoscopy. That rule comes from the FDA warnings on mixing alcohol with midazolam and fentanyl, and from standard discharge guidance. Rehydrate first, because bowel prep leaves you low on fluid. If polyps were removed or a biopsy was taken, no national rule sets a longer alcohol wait. Ask your endoscopy team, keep drinking light, and watch for bleeding for 2 weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I have a glass of wine the night of my colonoscopy?

No. MedlinePlus advises avoiding alcohol for at least 24 hours after the test. The sedatives can still be active even when you feel awake, and alcohol adds to their effect.

Is it safe to drink beer the day after a colonoscopy?

The usual rule is at least 24 hours, so the next day is often allowed once you feel fully alert and no other limit applies. Drink water first, because bowel prep can leave you dehydrated. Follow your own discharge sheet if it says otherwise.

Does alcohol cause bleeding after polyp removal?

We found no guideline that names alcohol as a cause of bleeding after polyp removal. Research ties bleeding mostly to polyp size, number, location, and removal method. Doctors do often restrict aspirin and anti-inflammatory drugs, so ask your team about alcohol if a large polyp was removed.

Can I drink alcohol during colonoscopy prep?

No. The SUPREP Bowel Prep Kit label tells patients not to drink alcohol while taking the prep and until after the colonoscopy. The label also warns that the prep itself can cause serious fluid loss.

What if I drank alcohol too soon after my colonoscopy?

If you feel very drowsy, confused, or have trouble breathing, get emergency help right away. Otherwise, stop drinking, drink water, and rest with another adult nearby. Call your endoscopy team if you have questions.
