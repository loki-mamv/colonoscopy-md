How Soon Can You Fly After a Colonoscopy?

November 11, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: For most people, wait about 24 hours. The UK Civil Aviation Authority advises no air travel for 24 hours after a colonoscopy, because gas left in the colon expands about 30% at cabin altitude. Sedation also clouds judgment for the rest of the day. After a large polyp removal, ask your doctor first.

Is there an official waiting time before flying?

Only one source we found gives a number. The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) guidance for health professionals says that after a colonoscopy, where a large amount of gas goes into the colon, it is advisable to avoid air travel for 24 hours. The CAA gives the same 24-hour advice after laparoscopic surgery.

US sources are less specific. The CDC Yellow Book chapter on air travel tells people who had recent surgery, particularly inside the abdomen, to talk with their doctor before flying. It does not mention colonoscopy or give a set number of hours. We did not find a US gastroenterology guideline with a fixed waiting time either.

So 24 hours is a reasonable default for a routine exam. It is not a rule written for every case. Your endoscopy team knows what was done and can adjust it.

Why does sedation matter for travel day?

Most colonoscopies use sedation. The drugs affect memory and judgment for hours, even when you feel awake. The MedlinePlus colonoscopy discharge instructions say the medicines can change the way you think for the rest of the day. They tell patients not to drive, not to return to work, and not to make important work or legal decisions that day.

The FDA label for midazolam injection, a common sedative, goes further. It advises no driving or hazardous machinery until drowsiness has gone, or until 1 full day has passed, whichever is longer.

Air travel involves many small decisions: getting to the airport, security lines, gate changes, and carrying bags. You also need a responsible adult to get you home first. Our page on whether you need a ride home after a colonoscopy covers that rule in detail.

What does cabin pressure do to gas left in your colon?

To see the colon wall, the doctor inflates the colon through the scope. MedlinePlus notes that air goes in for a better view, and that you may feel bloated and pass gas more often over the day. Some of that gas is still inside you when you leave.

Airplane cabins are pressurized, but not to sea level. The CDC says US rules require cabin pressure equal to a maximum altitude of about 8,000 feet. At lower pressure, trapped gas takes up more space. The CAA states that intestinal gas expands by about 30% by volume at a cabin altitude of 8,000 feet.

For most people, that means more bloating and cramping. The CAA explains why it matters more after some procedures: stretching the lining of the gut can cause bleeding, and in extreme cases a tear. Many units now use carbon dioxide instead of room air. Carbon dioxide is rapidly absorbed from the colon and breathed out through the lungs, so less gas stays behind. No guideline we found shortens the 24-hour advice because of it. For tips on easing gas after the exam, see our page on pain and bloating after a colonoscopy.

Does polyp removal change the plan?

It can. Removing a polyp leaves a small wound in the colon wall. That wound can bleed later, after you have gone home. In its 2019 review of colonoscopy adverse events, the American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy (ASGE) says bleeding after polyp removal may happen right away or be delayed for up to 4 weeks.

Delayed bleeding is not common. A 2017 meta-analysis of 12 studies and 14,313 patients found a pooled rate of 1.5%. The risk was higher with:

  • Polyps larger than 10 mm (odds ratio 3.41)
  • Polyps in the right side of the colon (odds ratio 1.60)
  • Cardiovascular disease (odds ratio 1.55)
  • High blood pressure (odds ratio 1.53)

If high blood pressure is part of your history, hypertension.md explains how it is managed. A 2025 Delphi consensus statement calls delayed bleeding the most common adverse event after polyp removal, and notes that many cases stop on their own. Even so, a bleed on a plane or in a place without good care is harder to handle. MedlinePlus adds that your doctor may ask you to avoid lifting for up to 1 week after polyp removal, which matters if you plan to haul luggage.

No source we reviewed sets a waiting time for flying after polyp removal. The decision depends on the size and number of polyps and on your blood thinner plan. Ask before you leave the endoscopy unit. Our page on blood in stool after polyp removal explains what amount of bleeding is expected.

What does a safe travel timeline look like?

This timeline draws on the sources above. Your own discharge sheet comes first if it says something different.

  1. Before the procedure: Tell your endoscopy team about any flight in the next few weeks. Ask whether a polyp removal would change the plan.
  2. Procedure day: No flying. Sedation affects judgment for the rest of the day, and gas is still in the colon.
  3. About 24 hours after: This matches the CAA advice for a routine colonoscopy. Most people can fly if bloating has eased and they feel clear-headed.
  4. Days 1 to 7 after polyp removal: Follow any limit on lifting your doctor gives. Pack light or get help with bags.
  5. Up to 4 weeks after polyp removal: Delayed bleeding remains possible in this window, per ASGE. Before a long or remote trip, ask your doctor if the risk is low enough.

When should you not get on the plane?

Do not fly, and call your doctor, if you have any of the warning signs MedlinePlus lists after a colonoscopy:

  • Black, tarry stools
  • Red blood in your stool, or blood in the stool for more than 2 bowel movements
  • Severe pain or cramps in your belly
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or vomiting blood
  • Chest pain
  • Chills or a fever over 101°F (38.3°C)

Pain that grows instead of easing is a special concern. The ASGE review notes that a perforation, though rare, may show up immediately or up to several days after the exam. Our page on how common bowel perforation is covers the signs. Bloating that is getting worse, not better, is a reason to stay on the ground until a doctor checks you.

If symptoms start mid-trip, tell the flight crew. Then seek care when you land and bring your discharge paperwork.

The bottom line

For a routine colonoscopy, plan to wait about 24 hours before flying. That matches UK aviation guidance and gives the sedation time to wear off. US agencies do not set a fixed number and defer to your doctor. If you had polyps removed, especially large ones, ask your doctor about travel in the following weeks. Any bleeding, severe pain, or fever means do not fly until a doctor checks you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I fly home the same day as my colonoscopy?

UK aviation guidance advises waiting 24 hours after a colonoscopy because of the gas put into the colon. Sedation also affects your judgment for the rest of the day. If a same-day flight cannot be moved, ask your endoscopy team before the procedure.

Is a long-haul flight different from a short flight?

No source we reviewed gives a separate rule for flight length. A long flight does put you farther from medical care if bleeding or pain starts. That matters most in the weeks after a large polyp is removed.

Does carbon dioxide instead of air make flying easier?

Carbon dioxide is absorbed from the colon quickly and breathed out through the lungs, so less gas stays trapped. No guideline we found shortens the 24-hour advice for it. Ask your unit which gas they use if you plan to travel soon.

Should I take my discharge paperwork on the trip?

Yes, it is a good idea. A doctor at your destination will want to know what was removed and which drugs you received. Note your endoscopy unit's phone number before you leave.

Do blood thinners change when I can fly?

Restarting blood thinners can affect the risk of delayed bleeding after polyp removal. A 2022 guideline could not say whether a same-day restart is better than a restart 1 to 7 days later. Your doctor will tell you when to restart, and that plan should guide how far you travel.
