Can You Take Ibuprofen After a Colonoscopy With Polyp Removal?

November 5, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Ask your endoscopy team first, and follow your discharge sheet. We found no national guideline that sets a fixed wait. The 2016 ASGE guideline says low doses of aspirin and NSAIDs may be continued around endoscopy, yet the clinic sheets we read range from 3 days to 14 days.

How long should you wait to take ibuprofen after polyp removal?

No single number exists in the guidelines we could read. The 2022 ACG-CAG guideline on blood thinners never mentions ibuprofen or NSAIDs. The ASGE guideline covers NSAIDs around endoscopy but gives no restart date. The recommendations published in the abstract of the 2024 ESGE polypectomy guideline do not list one either. We could not open the full text of that guideline or the 2020 US Multi-Society Task Force guideline, so either may say more.

Local discharge sheets fill the gap, and they disagree. These are examples, not rules:

  • 3 to 4 days. A Loudoun-area endoscopy practice sheet says to avoid aspirin and NSAIDs, including ibuprofen, unless your physician says otherwise.
  • 7 to 10 days, if your doctor recommends it. A University Colon & Rectal Surgery sheet hosted by the University of Tennessee Medical Center covers aspirin and arthritis medicines.
  • 14 days. A Boulder Medical Center aftercare form says no aspirin or ibuprofen products for 14 days if a polyp was removed.
  • No number. An East Hawaii clinic sheet says your provider may have you hold aspirin, ibuprofen, or NSAIDs to avoid bleeding.

MedlinePlus gives no number at all. It says: "Ask your doctor which medicines or blood thinners you should start taking again and when to take them." If your own sheet names a period, that period is your answer. If it says nothing about pain relievers, call the unit before your first dose.

Why might your doctor say to avoid ibuprofen after polyp removal?

Removing a polyp leaves a wound in the colon wall. NIDDK says light bleeding is normal, and that delayed bleeding can occur up to 2 weeks after the procedure. A 2021 review puts recent delayed bleeding rates at 0.3% to 1.2%.

Ibuprofen is an NSAID, and its over-the-counter label warns of severe stomach bleeding. That label does not mention colon wounds. The ASGE guideline does list NSAIDs among drugs to weigh around endoscopy. Its table gives aspirin a 7 to 10 day duration of action, and says NSAIDs vary.

The evidence on bleeding after polyp removal is mixed and thin:

  • A 2015 meta-analysis found higher odds of delayed bleeding (odds ratio 1.7), but the result lost significance when one small study was removed. Its authors concluded that aspirin or NSAIDs do not raise the risk.
  • A 2018 meta-analysis of 11 studies and 9,307 patients found no rise in immediate bleeding, but a higher chance of delayed bleeding (odds ratio 1.7).
  • A New Zealand study of 493 patients included only 16 NSAID users, and one bled. The odds ratio was 2.82, with a confidence interval of 0.34 to 23.3. That is too few people to settle anything.

The two meta-analyses group aspirin with NSAIDs, and they study people exposed around the procedure. We found no trial that tested when to restart ibuprofen. So a hold is a precaution, not a proven benefit. Our guide to blood in stool after polyp removal covers how much bleeding is normal.

Does polyp size, removal method, or a clip change the answer?

It can change how careful your team is. But no source we read gives a different ibuprofen wait by size, method, or clip. The sources do show which wounds bleed more:

  • Size. The 2021 review cites a meta-analysis in which polyps over 10 mm had 3.41 times the odds of delayed bleeding.
  • Location. The same review names the right colon, especially the cecum, as a risk factor.
  • Removal method. The ACG-CAG guideline says bleeding risk is higher with larger polyps and with cautery rather than a cold snare. In one screening program cited in the review, delayed bleeding for 4 to 10 mm polyps was 0.1% with cold snare and 1.1% with hot snare.
  • Clips. ESGE recommends clip closure after endoscopic mucosal resection of large polyps in the right colon. The 2025 CLIPPER trial of 356 patients found no reduction: delayed bleeding was 9.0% with clips and 6.1% without.

A clip does not clear you to take ibuprofen. No source we read says it does. If your report lists a large polyp, mucosal resection, or a clip, ask about pain relievers by name.

What can you take for pain instead of ibuprofen?

Acetaminophen is the usual alternative on the sheets that name one. The Loudoun-area sheet allows Tylenol for minor aches and pains. The Boulder form says acetaminophen is OK.

It has its own limits. The Tylenol Extra Strength label warns of severe liver damage with more than 4,000 mg in 24 hours, with other acetaminophen products, or with 3 or more alcoholic drinks a day. It says to ask a doctor first if you have liver disease. It says to ask a doctor or pharmacist if you take warfarin.

Our post on pain and bloating after colonoscopy explains what is normal. Severe belly pain is different. MedlinePlus lists it as a reason to contact your provider, so call instead of treating it at home.

What if you take a prescribed NSAID or daily aspirin?

Do not stop or restart a prescribed drug on your own. Your endoscopy team and your prescriber should agree on the plan.

  • Prescribed NSAIDs. We found no guideline date for these. ASGE says low doses may be continued around endoscopy, but some sheets above restrict them after polyp removal. Ask which instruction applies.
  • Daily aspirin for your heart. The ACG-CAG guideline suggests against interrupting aspirin 81 mg to 325 mg a day for secondary cardiovascular prevention. That advice is conditional, with very low certainty evidence. It does not cover aspirin taken for pain.
  • Aspirin plus ibuprofen. The ibuprofen label says to ask a doctor or pharmacist first, because ibuprofen may decrease the heart benefit of aspirin.
  • Blood thinners. The label says stomach bleeding is more likely with an anticoagulant. The ACG-CAG panel could not recommend for or against restarting warfarin or a DOAC the same day versus 1 to 7 days later. Ask for your restart date before you leave.
  • High blood pressure. The label says to ask a doctor before use if you have high blood pressure, heart disease, kidney disease, or a past stroke. hypertension.md has more on blood pressure.

For what to hold before the exam, see medications to stop before a colonoscopy.

What should you check before taking ibuprofen?

We built this list from the sources above. Your discharge sheet wins if it says something different.

  1. Read the medicine section of your discharge sheet. Look for ibuprofen, NSAIDs, aspirin, or arthritis medicines, and a number of days.
  2. Find your procedure report. Note the polyp size, the location, the removal method, and any clips.
  3. Ask for an end date. A date is harder to misread than "a few days."
  4. List everything you take. Include blood thinners, aspirin, steroids, and prescribed NSAIDs, which all appear in the label's bleeding warning.
  5. Check the label's other risk factors. These include age 60 or older, past ulcers or bleeding problems, and 3 or more alcoholic drinks a day.
  6. Try acetaminophen if your sheet allows it. Ask first if you have liver disease or take warfarin.
  7. Rule out warning signs. MedlinePlus says to contact your provider for black, tarry stools, blood in your stool for more than 2 bowel movements, severe belly pain, or fever over 101°F. NIDDK says to get care right away for bleeding that does not stop, dizziness, or weakness.

The bottom line

We found no national rule for ibuprofen after polyp removal. The clinic sheets we read run from 3 days to 14 days, and the evidence is mixed. Follow your own discharge sheet. If it is silent, call your endoscopy team before the first dose, and ask whether acetaminophen suits you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take Advil after a colonoscopy if no polyps were removed?

The sheets we read tie the ibuprofen hold to polyp removal or biopsy, so it may not apply to you. One clinic sheet says you can otherwise resume regular medicines as prescribed. Your own discharge sheet comes first.

Does a biopsy count the same as polyp removal for ibuprofen?

Two of the sheets we read apply the same hold after a biopsy or a polyp removal. The ACG-CAG guideline says bleeding risk is higher with polyp removal than with biopsy. Ask your team which rule fits your procedure.

Can I take naproxen or Aleve instead of ibuprofen?

Switching does not avoid the hold. The ibuprofen label groups naproxen with ibuprofen and aspirin as NSAIDs, and the Loudoun-area sheet names Aleve in its hold. Acetaminophen is the alternative that sheet allows.

What if I already took ibuprofen after my polyp was removed?

We found no source that addresses a single dose taken by mistake. Tell your endoscopy team and ask what to do next. Contact them for the warning signs MedlinePlus lists, such as black stools or blood in more than 2 bowel movements.

How long does the risk of bleeding last after polyp removal?

NIDDK says delayed bleeding can occur up to 2 weeks after the procedure. ESGE counts delayed bleeding up to 30 days after polyp removal, according to a 2021 review. Light bleeding right after removal is normal, NIDDK says.
