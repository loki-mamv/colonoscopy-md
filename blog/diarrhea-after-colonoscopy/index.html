Is It Normal to Have Diarrhea for Days After a Colonoscopy?

November 25, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Loose stools right after a colonoscopy are common, because the prep is a laxative and the bowel needs time to reset. A 2022 study found 45.5% of people had a new gut symptom that week. Diarrhea that is still frequent after several days, or comes with fever, blood, or severe pain, needs a call to your doctor.

Why are your stools loose after a colonoscopy?

The main cause is the bowel prep itself. Polyethylene glycol prep is an osmotic laxative. Per MedlinePlus, it "works by causing watery diarrhea so that the stool can be emptied from the colon." Some of that liquid can still be in the colon when the procedure ends, so the first trips to the bathroom are often watery.

The prep also flushes out gut bacteria. A 2019 study in Scientific Reports found that bowel prep sharply changed the gut microbiome right after the prep. The overall makeup was back to baseline within 14 days. Until it settles, stool form and timing can be irregular.

Gas adds to the effect. The colon is inflated during the exam, and MedlinePlus notes that you may "pass a lot of gas" with mild cramping afterward. Gas can push small, loose amounts of stool out with it. If cramping and bloating are your main problem, see our post on pain and bloating after a colonoscopy.

How common are bowel changes after the procedure?

New gut symptoms after colonoscopy are frequent. The numbers below come from published studies:

  • 45.5%: share of 793 people in a 2022 Italian study who reported at least one new gut symptom in the week after colonoscopy (Collatuzzo et al., Digestive and Liver Disease).
  • 32.2%, 17.9%, and 17.3%: the most common new symptoms in that study were bloating, indigestion, and abdominal pain.
  • 2.54 times: the higher odds of post-colonoscopy symptoms in women. People who had more symptoms while drinking the prep also had more symptoms afterward.
  • About 5% to 20%: share of patients who develop pain, bloating, or "bowel alteration" in the days and weeks after the procedure, per a 2025 review that links these symptoms mainly to prep-related changes in gut bacteria.

Diarrhea was not among the top three symptoms in the 2022 study. Bloating and pain were more common.

What does a normal recovery look like day by day?

This timeline combines guidance from NIDDK and the ASGE with the study data above. Your own pace can differ.

  1. Procedure day: Expect gas and cramping in the first hour, per NIDDK. Leftover prep liquid can make the first stools watery. Light bleeding is normal if polyps were removed or a biopsy was taken.
  2. Day 1: NIDDK says to expect a full recovery and a normal diet by the next day. Stools may still be soft or small. Some people have no stool at all, which is also common (see no bowel movement after a colonoscopy).
  3. Days 1 to 5: This is the window to watch for post-polypectomy electrocoagulation syndrome. The 2019 ASGE review says it typically appears 1 to 5 days after the procedure, with fever and localized belly pain.
  4. Days 1 to 7: In the 2022 study, new gut symptoms were tracked over this first week. The study did not track symptoms past this first week.
  5. Up to 14 days: NIDDK says delayed bleeding can occur up to 2 weeks after the procedure. The microbiome study found gut bacteria back to baseline by day 14.
  6. Up to 4 weeks: The ASGE review notes that bleeding after polyp removal can be delayed for up to 4 weeks.

When does diarrhea point to something else?

Loose stool that gets better each day is the expected pattern. Diarrhea that gets worse, or comes with other symptoms, can point to a complication:

  • Bleeding after polyp removal: NIDDK lists "bloody bowel movements that do not get better" and anal bleeding that does not stop as reasons to get care right away. In the ASGE review, bleeding occurred in 2.4 per 1,000 colonoscopies and was strongly tied to polypectomy. Our post on blood in stool after polyp removal covers what amount is normal.
  • Perforation: A tear in the colon wall is rare. The ASGE review found 5.8 perforations per 10,000 colonoscopies. Early signs are persistent belly pain and swelling, not diarrhea alone.
  • Burn injury after polyp removal: Fever and localized pain 1 to 5 days later can signal electrocoagulation syndrome, per the ASGE.
  • C. difficile infection: The CDC lists diarrhea, fever, stomach pain, loss of appetite, and nausea as symptoms. Risk is up to 10 times higher while taking an antibiotic and stays high for a month after.

Could it be C. diff or another infection?

Colonoscopy is not on the CDC's list of C. diff risk factors. The main risks are recent antibiotics, age 65 or older, a weak immune system, a past C. diff infection, and time in hospitals or nursing homes. The CDC also notes you can get it with none of these risk factors.

Infection from the colonoscopy itself is rare. The ASGE review says brief bacteria in the blood occur in about 4% of procedures, but signs of infection are rare. MedlinePlus lists infection needing antibiotics as a "very rare" risk.

So if you were given antibiotics around the time of your colonoscopy, new watery diarrhea deserves a call to your doctor. The CDC advises contacting a health care professional if you have recently taken antibiotics and develop symptoms.

How do you avoid dehydration while stools are loose?

You lose a lot of fluid during prep, and loose stools afterward add to it. MedlinePlus lists diarrhea as a leading cause of dehydration. Adult signs include strong thirst, dry mouth, dark urine, tiredness, and dizziness. Get help right away for confusion, fainting, no urine, or a racing heartbeat.

Drinking fluids and returning to regular meals, as NIDDK advises, helps your bowel and your energy recover. If you feel drained the next day, our post on feeling tired the day after a colonoscopy explains why. To check whether other symptoms fit a pattern, a symptom checker such as symptom.md can help you sort what to tell your doctor.

The bottom line

Loose stools right after a colonoscopy are expected. The prep is a laxative that works by causing watery diarrhea, and gut bacteria take up to 2 weeks to recover. Most people eat normally by the next day. Call your doctor for diarrhea that gets worse after several days, any fever, severe belly pain, ongoing bleeding, or signs of dehydration. Call promptly if you took antibiotics recently.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days of diarrhea after a colonoscopy is too many?

Leftover prep usually causes loose stools on the procedure day, and NIDDK expects most people to eat normally by the next day. In a 2022 study, 45.5% of people reported a new gut symptom during the first week. Diarrhea that is getting worse after several days, or comes with fever, blood, or strong pain, should be reported to your doctor.

Can bowel prep upset my gut bacteria for weeks?

Yes, for a short time. A 2019 study found that bowel prep sharply changed the gut microbiome right after the prep. The overall makeup was back to baseline within 14 days.

Is watery diarrhea with fever after a colonoscopy an emergency?

Fever is not part of normal recovery. NIDDK lists fever and severe belly pain as reasons to get care right away. Fever with localized pain 1 to 5 days after polyp removal can signal a burn injury to the bowel wall.

Should I worry about C. diff after my colonoscopy?

The CDC does not list colonoscopy as a C. diff risk factor. The biggest risk is antibiotic use, which raises risk up to 10 times and stays high for a month after. If you took antibiotics recently and develop watery diarrhea, call your doctor.

Is blood mixed with loose stool normal after polyp removal?

NIDDK says light bleeding is normal after polyp removal or a biopsy. Bloody stools that do not get better, or bleeding that does not stop, need care right away. The ASGE notes bleeding after polyp removal can be delayed for up to 4 weeks.
