Can You Have a Colonoscopy While You Are on Your Period?

Does a period affect a colonoscopy?

No, according to the hospital instructions we read. The scope goes through the rectum into the colon. Menstrual blood comes from the uterus and leaves through the vagina.

Three US hospital documents give the same answer:

  • Lahey Hospital & Medical Center: "Yes, having your period will not affect your test."
  • MedStar Health: "Your menstrual period will not interfere with your physician's ability to complete your procedure."
  • A colonoscopy FAQ hosted by Northwestern Medicine asks if a period will interfere and answers, "Not at all."

Be clear about the strength of this evidence. These are patient instruction sheets. They show what endoscopy units do. They are not trial results. Our PubMed searches found no study that compared colonoscopy during a period with colonoscopy at another time. The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) colonoscopy page does not mention periods at all.

Can you wear a tampon, a menstrual cup, or a pad during the procedure?

A tampon is the product that the instructions agree on. The two documents that discuss products differ about pads:

  • Lahey: "A tampon can be left in place during the test, but a pad would have to be removed." It adds that you can wear a tampon on the day of the test if that is more comfortable.
  • The Northwestern-hosted FAQ: "It is absolutely fine to use a tampon and/or pad."

Neither document mentions menstrual cups or discs. We did not find a hospital instruction about them, so we cannot tell you what your unit allows. Ask the unit before the day.

These steps follow from the instructions above:

  1. Tell the nurse at check-in that you have your period and which product you use.
  2. Ask if the unit wants a tampon, a pad, or neither during the test.
  3. Bring spare products for the recovery area and the trip home.

Is bowel prep harder during a period?

The prep itself does not change. NIDDK says the prep uses laxatives, often taken the night before and the morning of the procedure. It causes diarrhea, so stay near a bathroom. NIDDK says to call a health care professional if side effects keep you from finishing the prep.

We found no study on period cramps during bowel prep, so we will not give a number for it. Two points from the hospital instructions do apply:

  • Fluids. Lahey says to drink a lot of clear liquids the day before the test. It says to avoid alcohol, which makes you dehydrated.
  • Iron tablets. Some people take iron because of heavy periods. MedStar says iron can cause dark-colored stools, which can make the examination difficult if the prep is less than optimal. MedStar recommends that its patients stop oral iron at least one week before the procedure. Follow the instruction from your own unit, and ask the doctor who prescribed the iron.

Ask your unit which pain medicine you can take for cramps. Our article on medications to stop before a colonoscopy covers medicine questions. Our complete prep guide covers the full schedule.

Should you reschedule your colonoscopy because of your period?

There is no medical reason to do so in the instructions we read. MedStar asks the question directly: "Should I reschedule my colonoscopy appointment?" Its answer is "No."

A delay has a cost. You must repeat the diet changes and the prep, and you wait longer for an answer.

Some people still prefer a different date for comfort or privacy. That is a personal decision. If you want to move the date, call the unit early and ask how long the wait is.

Will you need a pregnancy test before sedation?

Possibly. A period does not always remove the question, and policies differ between units.

The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) has a statement on pregnancy testing before anesthesia and surgery. It was last amended on October 13, 2021. It says pregnancy testing "may be offered to female sex patients of childbearing age" and that "testing should not be mandatory." The ASA says the patient has the right to decide, and that staff should obtain informed consent or assent.

The same statement says no currently used anesthetic agent has been shown to have teratogenic effects in humans. It also says the scientific literature is inadequate to tell patients if anesthesia causes harmful effects.

Tell the unit if you are pregnant or could be. The Northwestern-hosted FAQ says a colonoscopy should not be performed during pregnancy "except in very rare cases." The American Society for Gastrointestinal Endoscopy has a separate guideline for endoscopy in pregnant and lactating women.

Does menstrual blood affect a stool test such as FIT?

This is where a period does change the plan. A fecal immunochemical test (FIT) looks for hidden blood in a stool sample. Menstrual blood can get into the sample during collection.

An NHS patient leaflet for FIT (London region, November 2018) is direct: "Do not do the test when you are menstruating (having your period), wait until it has finished and then do the test."

The US page from MedlinePlus does not mention periods. It says FIT detects only human blood from the lower intestines and that you need no special preparation. So read the leaflet in your own kit, and ask the provider who ordered it if the leaflet is silent.

MedlinePlus says a positive FIT usually leads to other tests, including a colonoscopy. Our comparison of Cologuard and colonoscopy accuracy explains how stool tests fit into screening.

Do heavy periods or endometriosis change the picture?

They change the reason for the test more than the date.

Heavy periods and iron deficiency. MedlinePlus lists heavy, long, or frequent menstrual periods as a common cause of iron loss. It also lists cancer in the esophagus, stomach, small bowel, or colon. Colonoscopy is one of the tests a provider may order to find the source of blood loss.

Two studies show why doctors sometimes look at the gut even when periods are heavy:

  • A 2009 study in Digestive Diseases and Sciences reviewed 168 premenopausal women who had endoscopy for iron deficiency anemia. Significant lower gastrointestinal lesions were found in 6 of the 155 women who had a colonoscopy (3.9%).
  • A 2008 study in Alimentary Pharmacology & Therapeutics followed 187 premenopausal women with iron deficiency anemia. Heavy menstrual bleeding and a possible gastrointestinal cause were present together in about one-third of the women.

Endometriosis. MedlinePlus says endometriosis tissue can attach to the bowel and the rectum. Its symptom list includes painful periods and pain with bowel movements. People with these symptoms sometimes ask for a date away from their period, when pain is lower. We found no guideline that sets a best time in the cycle, so this is a comfort preference to discuss with your doctor.

A biopsy during colonoscopy does not always confirm bowel endometriosis. A 2011 study of 17 patients with colorectal endometriosis found that biopsy during colonoscopy confirmed the diagnosis in 8 of them (47%). Our sibling site symptom.md covers pelvic pain and other symptoms that need a medical visit.

The bottom line

You can have a colonoscopy during your period. The hospital instructions we read say it does not interfere with the test and is not a reason to reschedule. A tampon can usually stay in place, but ask your unit about pads and menstrual cups. Expect a question about pregnancy, and possibly an offer of a test. If you have a stool test to do, wait until your period ends if the kit leaflet says so. All of this rests on facility instructions and not on trials, so your own unit's instruction comes first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the doctor see menstrual blood during the colonoscopy?

The scope examines the colon through the rectum, and menstrual blood leaves through the vagina. Hospital instructions from Lahey and MedStar say a period does not affect the test or the physician's ability to complete it. These are facility instructions and not trial results.

Do I need to tell the endoscopy staff that I have my period?

Yes, tell the nurse at check-in. The staff can tell you if the unit wants a tampon, a pad, or neither during the test. Lahey says a tampon can stay in place but a pad would have to be removed, and other units differ.

Can I keep a menstrual cup in during a colonoscopy?

The hospital instructions we read discuss tampons and pads but do not mention menstrual cups or discs. Ask your endoscopy unit before the day of the test. Bring a spare product in case the unit asks you to change.

Should I stop iron tablets before a colonoscopy?

MedStar tells its patients to stop oral iron at least one week before the procedure, because iron can cause dark-colored stools. Units differ, so follow your own written instructions. Ask the doctor who prescribed the iron before you stop it.

Can I do a FIT stool test while I have my period?

An NHS leaflet for FIT says not to do the test when you are menstruating and to wait until the period has finished. The MedlinePlus page on FIT does not mention periods. Read the leaflet in your kit, and ask the provider who ordered the test if you are not sure.
