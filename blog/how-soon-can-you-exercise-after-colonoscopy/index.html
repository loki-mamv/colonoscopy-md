How Soon Can You Exercise After a Colonoscopy?

November 14, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida
Table of Contents

  2. When can you work out if no polyps were removed?

The short answer: After a colonoscopy with no polyps removed, most people can return to normal exercise the next day. NIDDK expects a full recovery by then. If a polyp was removed, MedlinePlus (reviewed July 2025) says your doctor may limit lifting and other activity for up to 1 week. Large polyp removals can call for 2 weeks.

Can you exercise on the day of the procedure?

No, not in the usual sense. Most colonoscopies use sedation, and it takes the rest of the day to clear. The MedlinePlus colonoscopy discharge instructions say the medicines can change the way you think for the rest of the day. They tell patients not to drive, not to handle tools or equipment, and not to return to work that day.

The FDA label for midazolam injection, a common sedative for this test, is more specific. It advises no driving or hazardous machinery until drowsiness has gone, or until 1 full day has passed, whichever is longer. A barbell, a road bike, or a treadmill at speed falls in the same category: balance and reaction time matter.

Gentle walking is the exception. MedlinePlus lists walking around as one way to ease the gas and bloating from air pumped into the colon. A slow walk around the house or the block, with someone nearby, is fine for most people. If cramping is your main problem, our page on pain and bloating after a colonoscopy has more tips.

When can you work out if no polyps were removed?

For a look-only exam, or one with a few small biopsies, recovery is fast. NIDDK says you should expect a full recovery and a return to your normal diet by the next day. The sedation limit on the midazolam label also ends after 1 full day.

So the next day, most people can go back to their usual routine, including runs, classes, and weights. We found no society guideline that sets a longer wait after an exam with no polyp removal. Start a little easier than normal if you still feel tired or bloated.

How does polyp removal change the timeline?

Removing a polyp leaves a small wound in the colon wall. Doctors often limit heavy effort while it heals, to lower the chance of bleeding. MedlinePlus says that if a polyp was removed, your doctor may ask you to avoid lifting and other activities for up to 1 week.

How long you wait depends mostly on two things:

  • Polyp size. In a 2017 meta-analysis of 12 studies and 14,313 patients, polyps larger than 10 mm had about 3.4 times the odds of delayed bleeding. The pooled delayed bleeding rate was 1.5%.
  • How it was removed. In a 2023 randomized trial of 4,270 people with 4 to 10 mm polyps, delayed bleeding within 14 days occurred in 0.4% after cold snare removal and 1.5% after hot snare removal. Cold snare uses no electric current.

Large, flat polyps are often removed by endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR), which leaves a bigger wound. The 2019 ASGE review of colonoscopy adverse events found a pooled delayed bleeding rate of 4.0% after EMR. In a 2019 trial of EMR for lesions 2 cm or larger, delayed bleeding within 15 days reached 12.1% when the wound was not closed with clips.

One hospital gives a firm number for larger polyps. The United Lincolnshire Hospitals NHS Trust polypectomy leaflet (February 2026) says that after removal of polyps bigger than 1 cm, healing can take up to 2 weeks. It tells patients to avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise during this time, including work, sport, and heavy housework. That is one hospital's instruction, not a national standard.

No study we found tested exercise limits directly. These time frames come from discharge practice and from when bleeding tends to happen, not from a trial of runners or lifters.

How long does the bleeding risk last?

Longer than most people expect. The ASGE review says bleeding after polyp removal may happen right away or be delayed for up to 4 weeks. NIDDK describes delayed bleeding up to 2 weeks after the procedure.

In a prospective Korean study of 3,887 patients, 3.4% had some delayed bleeding, and 1.0% bled later than 24 hours after the procedure. People younger than 50 had higher odds of both major and late bleeding. Younger, active patients should not assume they are low risk.

Other factors that raised risk in the 2017 meta-analysis were polyps on the right side of the colon, heart disease, and high blood pressure. If your blood pressure runs high, hypertension.md covers how it is managed and how exertion affects it. A 2025 expert consensus calls delayed bleeding the most common adverse event after polyp removal, and notes that many cases stop on their own.

What does a day-by-day return-to-exercise timeline look like?

This timeline draws on the sources above. Your own discharge sheet comes first if it says something different.

  1. Procedure day: Rest. Short, easy walks help with gas. No gym, no cycling, no driving, per MedlinePlus and the midazolam label.
  2. Day 1, no polyps removed: NIDDK expects a full recovery by the next day, so most people can return to their usual activity unless the discharge sheet says otherwise. Ease in if you feel tired.
  3. Days 1 to 7, small polyps removed: Walking and light activity are usually fine. Ask before heavy lifting, intense intervals, or contact sports. MedlinePlus says the limit can last up to 1 week.
  4. Days 1 to 14, polyps over 1 cm or EMR: One NHS hospital advises no heavy lifting or strenuous exercise for 2 weeks. Ask your doctor whether clips were placed and what limit applies to you.
  5. Up to 4 weeks after any polyp removal: Delayed bleeding is still possible, per ASGE. Return to full training, but know the warning signs below.

Ask about your timeline before you leave the endoscopy unit, while your doctor still has the details of size, number, and removal method. You can also ask about pain relievers. Our page on blood in stool after polyp removal explains which amount of bleeding is expected.

When should you stop exercising and call your doctor?

Stop and call your doctor if you notice any of these, which come from the MedlinePlus list and the NHS leaflet:

  • Red blood in your stool for more than 2 bowel movements, or fresh blood or clots of more than a few tablespoons
  • Black, tarry stools
  • Severe pain or cramps in your belly that do not improve, or severe swelling of your belly
  • Fever over 101°F (38.3°C) or chills
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or chest pain
  • Feeling generally unwell, with flu-like symptoms

Pain with fever deserves attention even without bleeding. The ASGE review describes post-polypectomy syndrome, a burn injury to the bowel wall that usually shows up 1 to 5 days after the procedure with fever and local belly pain.

The bottom line

Skip workouts on procedure day, but a short walk is fine. With no polyps removed, most people can exercise normally the next day. After polyp removal, expect a limit on lifting and hard exercise of up to 1 week, or up to 2 weeks for large polyps, and watch for bleeding for up to 4 weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I go for a walk after my colonoscopy?

Yes, for most people. MedlinePlus lists walking around as one way to ease gas and bloating after the test. Keep it short and slow on procedure day, and have someone nearby while the sedation wears off.

Can I lift weights the day after a colonoscopy?

If no polyps were removed, most people can return to normal training the next day. If polyps were removed, MedlinePlus says your doctor may ask you to avoid lifting for up to 1 week. Ask before you leave the endoscopy unit.

Does a biopsy limit exercise the same way as polyp removal?

NIDDK says light bleeding after a biopsy or polyp removal is normal. MedlinePlus ties its lifting limit to polyp removal, not to biopsies. Your discharge sheet will say if your doctor wants any limit.

Is cycling or running harder on the colon than walking after polyp removal?

No study has compared types of exercise after polyp removal. The NHS leaflet cited above groups heavy lifting and strenuous exercise together. Walking is the safest choice until your doctor clears you for more.

What if I start bleeding during a workout a week after polyp removal?

Stop, rest, and call your endoscopy team. ASGE says delayed bleeding can happen up to 4 weeks after polyp removal. Go to an emergency department if the bleeding is heavy, there are clots, or you feel generally unwell.
