Is It Normal to Feel Tired the Day After a Colonoscopy?

November 10, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Yes. Mild tiredness the next day is common and usually fades within a day. Sedation drugs, fluid lost to the bowel prep, and a broken night all play a part. A 2018 meta-analysis of 14 trials found that split-dose preps disturb sleep more. Dizziness or real weakness is a reason to call.

Why do people feel tired the day after a colonoscopy?

Most people blame the procedure itself. But the colonoscopy is short. The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) says it usually takes less than one hour. The tiredness tends to come from everything around it.

Three causes have good support in drug labels and research:

  • Sedation. The drugs that keep you comfortable take hours to clear, and drowsiness is a listed effect.
  • Fluid loss from the prep. A bowel prep causes heavy diarrhea, and feeling tired is a known symptom of dehydration.
  • Lost sleep. Many preps split the dose so that the second half falls early on the morning of the test.

You also eat very little for about a day. The prep instructions limit you to a light breakfast or clear liquids the day before. Each factor is small. Together they can leave you flat for a day.

How long do the sedation drugs stay in your system?

NIDDK says a health care professional gives sedatives, anesthesia, or pain medicine through an IV. Midazolam is a common sedative, and its label lists endoscopic procedures as an approved use. Propofol is another option. Our guide to being awake for a colonoscopy explains the options.

The FDA-approved label for midazolam injection gives an elimination half-life of 1.8 to 6.4 hours in healthy adults, with a mean of about 3 hours. That means some of the drug is still present well into the evening. The label advises no driving or hazardous machinery until effects such as drowsiness have gone, or until 1 full day has passed, whichever is longer. People with acute kidney failure may clear it more slowly and recover later.

Propofol wears off quickly, but the drugs given with it can still add to fatigue. In a 2026 randomized trial of 248 patients sedated with propofol plus an opioid, 35.7% of the sufentanil group had clinically significant fatigue 30 minutes after the procedure. The rate was 20.8% in the alfentanil group. The trial measured fatigue only in the first hour, so it does not tell us about the next day.

Can the bowel prep leave you drained and dehydrated?

Yes, if you do not replace what you lose. The FDA label for SUPREP Bowel Prep Kit tells all patients to hydrate well before, during, and after the prep. It warns that fluid and electrolyte problems can lead to serious events, including heart rhythm problems, seizures, and kidney injury. The warning shows how much fluid a prep can pull.

The same label lists signs of too much fluid loss: vomiting, urinating less often than normal, dizziness, and headache. MedlinePlus adds that dehydration in adults can cause thirst, dry mouth, dark urine, feeling tired, and dizziness. So a heavy, washed-out feeling the next day can partly be a fluid deficit. If the prep itself went badly, see our page on vomiting during colonoscopy prep.

NIDDK says to expect a return to your normal diet by the next day. Eating regular meals and drinking fluids helps you catch up.

Does a split-dose prep cost you sleep?

Often, yes. With a split-dose prep, you drink half the evening before and half the morning of the test. The SUPREP label sets the second dose in the morning, 10 to 12 hours after the evening dose. Each dose is followed by extra water over the next hour. NIDDK notes the prep causes diarrhea, so you need to stay close to a bathroom. That makes a full night of sleep hard.

A 2018 meta-analysis in the Journal of Clinical Gastroenterology pooled 14 randomized trials. Same-day preps and split-dose preps cleaned the bowel about equally well (79.4% vs 81.7% adequate). People who took a same-day prep slept better the night before. The authors note that split-dose has been the recommended first choice, so do not change your plan without your doctor.

One short night does not usually cause lasting problems. If poor sleep goes on for weeks after the test, that is a separate issue. insomnia.md covers ongoing sleep trouble.

How long does the tiredness usually last?

For most people, about a day. NIDDK says you will stay at the center for about 30 to 60 minutes while the sedatives wear off. It says you should expect a full recovery by the next day.

A 2011 study in Digestive Diseases and Sciences asked 68 working adults about time off after a mid-week screening colonoscopy. 10% took the day after off, and 9% took both the day before and the day after. Of those who took the day after, 57% did so as a precaution after moderate sedation. The other 43% had symptoms.

A typical timeline, built from the sources above:

  1. Prep evening: clear liquids, the first dose, and frequent bathroom trips. Sleep is often broken.
  2. Procedure morning: the second dose 10 to 12 hours later (split-dose preps), then no fluids for at least 2 hours before the test, per the SUPREP label.
  3. First hour after: 30 to 60 minutes in recovery while the sedation wears off. Some cramping and bloating is normal.
  4. Rest of that day: drowsiness can linger. Do not drive. Eat, drink, and rest.
  5. Next day: most people feel back to normal and eat their usual diet. Some feel a little tired or slow.
  6. Day 2 and later: tiredness should be fading, not growing. Rising fatigue with other symptoms needs a call.

When is tiredness after a colonoscopy a warning sign?

Ordinary tiredness improves with food, fluids, and sleep. NIDDK lists the symptoms that need medical care right away after a colonoscopy:

  • severe pain in your abdomen
  • fever
  • bloody bowel movements that do not get better
  • bleeding from the anus that does not stop
  • dizziness
  • weakness

So tiredness alone is common, but dizziness or weakness is on the list for urgent care. NIDDK notes that delayed bleeding can happen up to 2 weeks after the procedure. Most bleeding happens in older people or after polyp removal. Our page on blood in stool after polyp removal covers this in detail.

Also call if you still have signs of dehydration, such as vomiting or passing much less urine than usual. A 2019 systematic review of 7 cohorts and 6,172 patients found that about one-third of patients report minor problems after colonoscopy. Abdominal pain and bloating were the most common, mostly in the first 1 to 2 weeks. Fatigue that is getting worse, not better, is worth a phone call to the endoscopy unit.

The bottom line

Feeling tired the day after a colonoscopy is normal for many people. Sedation, fluid loss from the prep, and a short night are the usual causes. NIDDK says most people recover fully by the next day. Eat, drink, and rest. Call your doctor if you have dizziness, weakness, fever, severe belly pain, or bleeding that does not stop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I take the day after my colonoscopy off work?

Most people do not need to. NIDDK says to expect a full recovery by the next day. In one small study of working adults, 10% took the day after off, and more than half of them did so only as a precaution.

Can I drive the day after a colonoscopy?

The midazolam label advises no driving until drowsiness has gone or until 1 full day has passed, whichever is longer. Follow the instructions your endoscopy team gives you, since the drugs used vary.

Is it normal to feel sleepy for the rest of the procedure day?

Yes. Midazolam has a half-life of about 3 hours on average and up to 6.4 hours in healthy adults. Drowsiness that lasts into the evening is expected.

Will drinking fluids help me feel less tired?

It can. The bowel prep causes heavy fluid loss, and MedlinePlus lists feeling tired and dizziness as signs of dehydration. Drinking fluids and eating normal meals helps you replace what you lost.

Does a same-day prep cause less fatigue than a split-dose prep?

A 2018 meta-analysis found that people slept better the night before with a same-day prep. It did not measure next-day tiredness directly. Your doctor picks the prep schedule, so ask before you change it.
