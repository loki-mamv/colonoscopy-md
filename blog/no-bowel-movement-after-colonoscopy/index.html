Why Haven't I Had a Bowel Movement Since My Colonoscopy?

The short answer: Your colon was emptied for the exam, so there is little stool left to pass. It takes time and food to refill. MedlinePlus colonoscopy discharge instructions (2026) say to contact your provider if you have no bowel movement for more than 3 to 4 days.

Why is there nothing to pass after a colonoscopy?

The prep is designed to clear out your colon. MedlinePlus explains that the colon must be completely empty for the exam, or problems can be missed. To get there, you drank laxatives and lived on clear liquids for the day before.

That means two things after the exam:

  • The tank is empty. The stool you would normally pass was flushed out during prep.
  • Refilling takes food. Clear liquids leave very little behind. Stool forms again only after you eat solid food for a while.

So a quiet day or two after a colonoscopy is usually a sign the prep worked, not a sign that something is wrong. If you want a refresher on what the prep does, see our complete colonoscopy prep guide.

How long does it usually take to have a bowel movement again?

We looked for a sourced figure and did not find one. No US society page we reviewed gives a standard number of hours or days for the first stool after a colonoscopy. The NIDDK colonoscopy page and the American College of Gastroenterology patient page do not state one either.

What the sources do give is an outer limit and a diet timeline:

  • NIDDK says you should expect a full recovery and a return to your normal diet by the next day.
  • MedlinePlus lists "no bowel movement for more than 3 to 4 days" as a reason to contact your health care provider.

Some hospital leaflets give their own windows, and they differ. Your own discharge sheet wins over any general figure, including this one.

What else can slow your first bowel movement?

An empty colon is the main reason. A few common habits after the exam can add to the delay. The NIDDK page on constipation causes lists several that apply here:

  • Not enough fiber. Many people eat soft, bland food for a day or two after the exam. That is gentle, but it gives the colon little to work with.
  • Not enough fluids. The prep can leave you a little dry. NIDDK names dehydration as a cause of constipation.
  • Certain medicines. NIDDK lists narcotic pain medicines, some antacids, diuretics, and iron supplements. If you got pain medicine with your sedation or took one afterward, ask your care team what it was.
  • A change in routine. NIDDK notes that bowel habits can shift when your routine changes or when you ignore the urge to go.

If constipation was already a problem for you, recovery may take longer. People who needed a longer prep for this reason can read about two-day prep for constipation. Ongoing hard stools can also have causes outside the gut. For example, MedlinePlus lists "hard stools or constipation" as an early symptom of an underactive thyroid, which thyroid.md covers in depth.

What should you expect day by day, and what should you do?

This timeline is built from the MedlinePlus and NIDDK pages cited in this article. It describes a typical outpatient exam without complications.

  1. Day of the exam. Expect gas and bloating. MedlinePlus says you may burp or pass gas more often over the day from air pumped into your colon. Start with small sips of water, then small amounts of solid food when that goes easily. No bowel movement today is common.
  2. Day 1 after. NIDDK says to expect a return to your normal diet by the next day. Eat regular meals. Drink water and other liquids. Walk if you feel up to it. Use the bathroom as soon as you feel the urge.
  3. Day 2 after. If you have not gone yet, keep eating normal meals and add fiber foods. NIDDK lists whole grains, beans, lentils, fruit, vegetables, and nuts. It says to add fiber a little at a time. Try sitting on the toilet 15 to 45 minutes after breakfast, which NIDDK suggests because eating helps the colon move stool.
  4. Day 3 after. If you still have not gone but feel well and can pass gas, call your endoscopy team during office hours. Ask them before you take a laxative. NIDDK says a health care professional may recommend one for a short time and can help you choose the right type.
  5. Day 4 after. This is past the "more than 3 to 4 days" point in the MedlinePlus instructions. Contact your provider if you have not done so already.

Any warning sign in the next section overrides this timeline, on any day.

When is no bowel movement a warning sign?

No stool alone, in someone who feels fine and is passing gas, is rarely an emergency. No stool with other symptoms is different. Two serious problems to rule out are a blockage and a tear in the colon wall.

MedlinePlus on intestinal obstruction says to contact a provider if you:

  • Cannot pass stool or gas
  • Have a swollen belly that does not go away
  • Keep vomiting

NIDDK says to seek medical care right away after a colonoscopy for severe belly pain, fever, bloody bowel movements that do not get better, bleeding from the anus that does not stop, dizziness, or weakness. MedlinePlus adds black, tarry stools, chest pain, and chills or a fever over 101°F (38.3°C).

A tear (perforation) is rare. NIDDK reports about 3 perforations in every 10,000 procedures in one screening study. Our article on how common bowel perforation is covers the symptoms and timing in more detail.

What if you are passing gas but no stool?

Passing gas means air is moving through your bowel. MedlinePlus lists "inability to pass gas" as a symptom of obstruction, so gas moving is reassuring. It does not replace the 3 to 4 day limit. If gas and cramping are your main problem, our guide to pain and bloating after colonoscopy explains what is normal.

The bottom line

A delay in your first bowel movement after a colonoscopy is expected. The prep emptied your colon, and stool needs solid food and time to build up again. Eat normally from the next day, drink fluids, add fiber slowly, and move around. Call your care team if you have not gone in more than 3 to 4 days, and ask before you take a laxative. Get care right away for severe belly pain, a swollen belly, vomiting, fever, or bleeding that does not stop.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal not to poop for two days after a colonoscopy?

It is common. The prep emptied your colon, and stool has to build up again from solid food. MedlinePlus advises contacting your provider if you have no bowel movement for more than 3 to 4 days, or sooner if you have warning signs.

Should I take a laxative if I have not had a bowel movement after my colonoscopy?

Ask your endoscopy team first. NIDDK says a health care professional may recommend a laxative for a short time and can help you choose the right type. Do not take one if you have severe belly pain, a swollen belly, or vomiting, and get care instead.

What should I eat to get my bowels moving after a colonoscopy?

NIDDK says most people return to a normal diet by the day after the exam. Add fiber foods such as whole grains, beans, fruit, and vegetables a little at a time, and drink water and other liquids with them.

Can sedation cause constipation after a colonoscopy?

NIDDK lists narcotic pain medicines among the drugs that can cause constipation. Sedation plans vary, so ask your care team which medicines you received. An empty colon after prep is still the most likely reason for a slow start.

When should I go to the ER for no bowel movement after a colonoscopy?

Get care right away if you cannot pass gas or stool and also have a swollen belly or keep vomiting. NIDDK also lists severe belly pain, fever, bleeding that does not stop, dizziness, and weakness as reasons to seek care right away.
